## Role

The subject is the design spec for **H-2, the networked duel**, in FTLite. FTLite is the owner's deterministic TypeScript/Electron/Pixi co-op space game. H-2 runs the gravity-well duel with two machines and one player each, over Net-3. It is the Helm demo the owner then playtests with another person.

H-2 builds on merged work:
- Net-3: WebRTC, Cloudflare signalling and TURN, join codes, recovery and the online sector.
- The engine refactor R-0 to R-2: a generic authority; the duel as a registered Net-3 mode with its own codec, snapshot, checkpoint and replay; N-ship slots; SIM 38.

The spec proposes three milestones:
- **H-2a**, the online duel over loopback and LAN: client codec and validation, a publication cache, seats and swap, the online duel view, an away steward, rematch rounds (SIM 39), telemetry records, tests, and an Electron smoke gate.
- **H-2b**: internet, a telemetry report, and a latency overlay.
- **H-2c**: own-ship prediction, only if the latency criteria trip.

New owner direction from 2026-11-16 applies: no couch co-op, no gamepads (keyboard and mouse only), and a main menu of Single player / Multiplayer (Host, Join) / Settings. The menu is being built separately, before H-2. The spec is written to assume it. Review the spec on its merits.

### [P0] R1-F1 Rematch as an in-place createDuel reset breaks the tick, checkpoint and init invariants it relies on
§7 resets the duel to `createDuel(layout, nextSeed(seed), init.helmPlayer)` inside the step and says the networking, checkpoint format and recovery stay unchanged. As written that cannot work. (1) createDuel starts the interior and tactical ticks at 0, and the authority tick is the interior tick, so the encounter tick goes backwards. The stream sender then drops every ordinary publication whose tick is not above the last one it sent. The client holds back events until publication.tick reaches the event's tick. The checkpoint requires ledger and result ticks to be at most the snapshot tick. (2) The duel checkpoint validator requires the snapshot seed to equal init.seed, so every checkpoint after a rematch fails, including the periodic baseline that executeTick builds every 300 ticks, and that failure faults the authority. (3) The validator requires a decided outcome if and only if phase === 'ended', so any capture during a decided-but-running round (pause, periodic, Net-3e mirror) also fails. Grace lengths are checked against the init seed's map. (4) `rules.step(state, input)` and `rules.restore(snapshot, resolveLayout, memory)` receive no init, so `init.helmPlayer` cannot be reached from the step, nor after a re-host restore. · spec L112, L99 ('checkpoint format does not change'), L111, L150 (restore mid-rematch test); state.ts:L43,L46 (fresh tactical and interior); duelRules.ts:L31 (tick = interior tick); sender.ts:L46 (`owned.tick <= lastOrdinaryTick` dropped); clientRun.ts:L155 (events wait for publication.tick ≥ event.tick); duelCheckpoint.ts:L91 (seed === init.seed), L92-93 (ended ⇔ phase 'ended'), L94, L97 (tick bounds), L98 (grace from init seed), L99; core.ts:L178, L258, L458 (resetBaseline → captureCheckpoint → validate inside executeTick); rules.ts:L39, L52 (no init in step or restore) · The first rematch or the first capture on an end card faults the host authority or freezes the remote view. The spec's 'networking stays untouched' claim and its recovery test cannot hold. · Specify round state explicitly. Keep the encounter tick monotone across a round: carry the tick into the new round, or add a round base tick. Hold `round`, the round seed and `helmPlayer` where the step and restore can read them: hashed duel state (then add a duel-oracle MIGRATIONS entry for the added fields) or modeMemory. Change validateDuelCheckpoint: seed = the round seed derived from init and round; only `phase === 'ended'` implies a decided outcome; grace lengths come from the round seed. List these as the duel checkpoint and memory schema changes under schemaVersion 2. Add tests that capture and restore on the end card and after a rematch.

### [P1] R1-F2 A decided round leaves commands without results and freezes the tick and the admission budget
Today a decided duel is phase 'ended', so the core rejects new commands at admission. Under §7 the phase stays running, but the step still returns [] before touching any command, and only `rematch` is handled ahead of that return. Every other command sent on the end card (Space, F, X, E, the second rematch) is queued, executed with no result, and left pending in the ledger. The next checkpoint export then throws, and the pending cap fills. The early return also skips `st.tick++`, so the tick stays fixed for the whole end card. The ledger's per-tick count therefore never resets: after 16 commands per player, the rematch itself is rejected over-capacity. Repeated same-tick publications are also dropped by the sender. The core's safe-point capture on the move to ended no longer fires. · spec L112-113, L149; step.ts:L73 (`return []`), L126 (tick advance skipped); core.ts:L366-368 (today's ended rejection), L447 (results only for executed), L139 (safe-point keyed on 'ended'); ledger.ts:L66-70 (count reset only when the tick changes), L98 ('cannot checkpoint pending ledger'); sender.ts:L46 · A planner has to invent what happens between rounds. The natural reading deadlocks the outbox and breaks checkpoints and mirrors on the end card. · Define decided-round semantics. Say whether ticks advance, and recommend that they do so that admission budgets, publications and checkpoints keep working. Give every non-rematch command a result: rejected with a named reason, `noop` for the second rematch. ### [P1] R1-F3 The design assumes 60 Hz publications, but Net-3 streams ordinary state at 20 Hz to both renderers
The streamed state sender sends an ordinary publication only every third tick, the Net-3 20 Hz decision. The host's own renderer is a streamed attachment too. The spec assumes one publication per tick throughout. `alpha()` is (now − receive time)·60/1000 clamped to 1, so it saturates 16.7 ms into a 50 ms interval and ships freeze for two frames of every three: stutter by design. 'The client receives 60 publications a second' is wrong. Host Helm latency of 35–60 ms leaves out up to 50 ms of cadence wait. The 'first remedy, a 1-tick buffer' cannot cover a 3-tick gap. Deriving effects from counters works over 3-tick gaps in which a missile can end and be relaunched. The Helm spec itself plans for interpolation over 20–30 Hz deltas. · spec L59, L78, L132-134, L183, L62; sender.ts:L46 (`owned.tick % 3 !== 0` → not sent); main/network/onlineSession.ts:L274 (host's own renderer `streamed: true`), L200; docs/.../2026-11-14-net-3-internet-sessions-design.md:L77 ('ordinary remote state sends at 20 Hz, every third completed tick'); helm-demo-design.md:L243 · Smoothness, the latency budget, the H-2c thresholds and the effects derivation all depend on the publication rate. Changing the sender for everyone would change Net-3 and sector behaviour. · Decide explicitly. Either (a) keep 20 Hz: interpolate over the actual tick gap between the previous and latest publication (alpha = elapsed / (Δtick·16.7 ms)), restate the expected latencies, budgets and remedies for 20 Hz, and check derived effects for slot reuse within one gap; or (b) add a per-mode cadence option to createModeStreamBinding/createStateSender, default 3 so the sector is unchanged and 1 for the duel, with a bandwidth and latestPayloadLimit check and a test that the sector cadence is unchanged.
