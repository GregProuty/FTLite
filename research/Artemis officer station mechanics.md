# Artemis stations teach FTLite how to split work

Artemis Spaceship Bridge Simulator works because every officer owns one slice of information and one slice of control, so no console can win alone and the crew has to talk. Science alone scans shield frequencies, Weapons alone fires and locks, Helm alone steers, Comms alone talks to ships and stations, and Engineering alone reallocates power, coolant and repair teams ([Artemis Manual v1.70 rev5](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf)). The open-source clone EmptyEpsilon (GPL, so only its documented mechanics are described here, never its code) keeps the same structure and adds merged stations for small crews ([EmptyEpsilon site](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/)). What transfers to FTLite is the structure rather than the interface: role-exclusive actions, a neutral-plus-boost power model with a shared cooling budget, damage as a node graph that blocks repair paths, request-driven callouts between stations, and the merge-by-information-flow rule for small crews. What does not transfer is separate-screen secrecy, a Captain with no console, slider-and-hotkey Engineering, and scripted mission or Game Master tooling. The evidence is uneven. The primary source is a 1.x manual (v1.70), the community wiki mixes versions, no developer interviews could be read, and several numbers (coolant tables, heat rates, repair rates, team counts) are missing or secondhand. The design conclusions below are therefore hypotheses to playtest at 1, 2, 3 and 4 players.

## Each Artemis station owns a distinct decision loop

**Version caveat.** The manual used is v1.70 rev5, a 1.x manual. Artemis 2.0 shipped in August 2013, the Fighter station arrived in 2.3.0, and the latest stable release is 2.8.0 (December 2021) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis:_Spaceship_Bridge_Simulator)). Everything below is 1.70 unless tagged otherwise, and 2.x changes to Comms fleet commands, Fighter and Engineering were not verified.

**Captain and main screen.** The Captain has no controls. The manual casts the role as decision-maker and coach, and the Captain need not know every job ([Manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf)). A reviewer saw the Captain "mostly talking and giving orders" ([Board Game Quest](https://www.boardgamequest.com/artemis-spaceship-bridge-simulator-review/)). Only Helm and Weapons can change the main-screen view, and Comms, not the Captain, toggles Red Alert ([Manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf)).

**Helm.** Helm has a warp slider (max Warp 4), an impulse slider, a rudder slider that must be re-centred or the ship keeps turning, click-to-set heading, reverse (new in 1.70), shields up/down (shared with Weapons), and docking within 600 units of a station. An optional jump drive has a countdown that cannot be cancelled once begun and whose length depends on Engineering power. Higher warp is faster but less efficient, and nebulae cap FTL at Warp 1. Docking refills energy, torpedoes and damage-control teams ([Manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf)). The reviewer rated Helm one of the better roles, busiest in battle, though the initial instructions were thin ([Board Game Quest](https://www.boardgamequest.com/artemis-spaceship-bridge-simulator-review/)).

**Weapons.** Weapons loads, unloads and fires tubes (one item per tube), retunes beams among 5 frequencies, toggles Auto Beams, and locks targets by clicking them. Without a lock, auto beams do not fire and torpedoes fly straight and hit the first thing they meet, friendlies included. Beams are instantaneous, use unlimited ammunition and hit only inside their arc. Deselecting Auto Beams allows manual targeting, which can punch through strong shields and disable specific enemy systems, but needs Helm to hold the arc and Engineering to keep beams charged. Ordnance is Homing, Nuke (1,000-unit blast), Mine and ECM, with ship-specific caps (Light Cruiser: 8 homing, 2 nukes, 6 mines, 4 ECM) ([Manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf)). Community figures for beam range (about 1000) and torpedo range (about 5000) came only through a search summary of the wiki FAQ and are unverified ([Artemis Wiki FAQ](http://artemiswiki.pbworks.com/w/page/39352331/FAQs)).

**Science.** Science selects a contact and scans. A first scan reveals class, strength and designation, and a second reveals shield frequency strengths, which identifies the weakest beam frequency to hand to Weapons. Science is the only station that sees anomalies, monsters, whales, nebulae and singularities, and the Engineering Tactical (sensors) setting speeds its scans ([Manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf)). It is the most interactive role in the review but idles between scans ([Board Game Quest](https://www.boardgamequest.com/artemis-spaceship-bridge-simulator-review/)).

**Communications.** Comms picks from fixed menus: Station, Enemy or Other Ship. "Stand by for docking" halves resupply time and doubles weapon load and refuel rate for 30 seconds. A station can be asked to build a specific ordnance type. Each enemy hears each of 3 taunts once, and a successful taunt makes it and its squadron chase the player ship blindly for 3 minutes. Friendly destroyers and transports can be redirected away from hazards ([Manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf)). Reviewers called the role menu-limited, predictable and repetitive by mid-to-late game ([Board Game Quest](https://www.boardgamequest.com/artemis-spaceship-bridge-simulator-review/)).

### The dependency web

Almost every combat action needs a handoff. Science scan to Weapons beam frequency. Helm heading to Weapons arc. Engineering power to beam rate of fire, torpedo load time, scan speed, turn rate, speed and shield recovery. Comms to Weapons and Engineering through station builds and docking resupply. Science hazard spotting to Comms rerouting of friendly ships ([Manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf)). The manual's own example orders show the intended loop ("Helm, set course two-seven-zero"; "what is that Skaraan's weakest shield frequency?"). A review makes the same point, that no console has enough data or control to operate alone ([Fistful of Wits](https://fistfulofwits.com/2013/07/10/artemis-bridge-simulator-offers-arch-entertainment/)).

### EmptyEpsilon mechanics

EmptyEpsilon (GPL; mechanics from its wiki only) has six positions: Captain/Main Screen, Helms, Weapons, Engineering, Science and Relay (sector map, probes, waypoints, comms). Helms and Weapons have the same kind of controls as Artemis's Helm and Weapons. Its Science scan is a minigame that aligns scanning frequencies, and a harder deep scan reveals shield and beam frequencies and individual system status. Weapons gets beams that auto-fire at a selected target inside arc and range, with frequency matching and subsystem targeting, plus missile tubes in sizes S, M and L ([Site](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/), [Science](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/daid/EmptyEpsilon/wiki/Science), [Weapon Types](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/daid/EmptyEpsilon/wiki/Weapon-Types)). Relay adds probes and hacking minigames whose grid size follows the difficulty setting ([Relay](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/daid/EmptyEpsilon/wiki/Relay)). Its heat model is broader than Artemis's: beam shots, jump, strafe and boost all generate heat, and jumping while overheated can damage the drive ([Helms](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/daid/EmptyEpsilon/wiki/Helms), [Weapons](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/daid/EmptyEpsilon/wiki/Weapons)). A community comparison sheet says Artemis has a Fighter station and interactive beam targeting while EmptyEpsilon has minigames, directional tubes and reduced-crew stations, but that sheet was not opened directly ([Comparison sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1L2eCCefJwOUbXCDRmPdmQHE0GeP4Fp3cg63b-TAPrc4/edit), [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2018/12/emptyepsilon-a-free-and-open-source-spaceship-bridge-sim/)). No EmptyEpsilon version is named on its site. The wiki pages read are dated around 2020, and the notes' fetch summaries were produced by a small model, so exact figures there should be rechecked before use.

## Engineering is a heat-versus-power dial with one shared coolant pool

This is the section most directly reusable for FTLite. Artemis Engineering has 8 system sliders: beams, torpedoes, sensors, maneuver, impulse, warp (or jump), and front and rear shields. 100% is neutral and needs no coolant. Above 100% a system performs better but drains energy faster and builds heat, and heat not countered by coolant eventually destroys a node of that system, "possibly injuring crew members in the vicinity". Below 100% a system sheds heat and saves energy. A system with no power does nothing useful, and below-neutral service is the shedding case ([Manual p.34-35](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf?t=1518791376)). Coolant is a constant-sum allocation: **eight coolant units shared by all systems**, never depleted and redistributable at any time. The decision is therefore where the pips go this minute, not how much coolant remains ([Manual](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf?t=1518791376), [Wiki-Eng](http://artemiswiki.pbworks.com/w/page/39352367/Engineering)).

**Energy as a clock.** All systems draw from one energy pool of about 999 to 1000, refilled by docking, anomalies, converting a loaded torpedo to 100 energy, or setting all sliders to zero so the auxiliary unit recovers about 0.8 energy per second ([Wiki-Energy](http://artemiswiki.pbworks.com/w/page/43660763/Energy), [Manual p.35](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf?t=1518791376)). Community-documented drain multipliers are steep for warp (x6), shields (x5 each) and impulse (x4), and scale with the square of the power multiplier, so 300% costs x9 ([Wiki-Eng](http://artemiswiki.pbworks.com/w/page/39352367/Engineering), [Wiki-Energy](http://artemiswiki.pbworks.com/w/page/43660763/Energy)). Community tables put sustainable overpower at roughly 100, 150, 190, 220 and 250% for 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8 coolant units, and the 300% slider ceiling comes from wiki tips, not the manual ([Wiki-Eng](http://artemiswiki.pbworks.com/w/page/39352367/Engineering)). Treat these as community approximations. The notes found no primary source for heat accumulation rate, overheat threshold, per-system multipliers, or whether heat is tracked per system or per node.

**Timing.** The community advice is that many boosts matter only at an instant: beams while firing, torpedo power only while loading, shields at the moment a hit lands. Skilled engineers therefore spike power briefly and reset ([Wiki-Eng](http://artemiswiki.pbworks.com/w/page/39352367/Engineering)). That is a community claim, not confirmed in the manual. Presets (Shift+number to save, number to recall) exist because individual slider moves are too slow in the heat of battle. That is the strongest evidence in the notes that real-time power control needs a few discrete states ([Wiki-Eng](http://artemiswiki.pbworks.com/w/page/39352367/Engineering), [Rigbook](https://cattail.nu/artemis/engineersRigbook/about.php)).

**Damage control.** Each of 8 systems is spread over 4 to 6 nodes with damage 0 to 100%. A damaged node works in proportion to its damage, and a system with all nodes at 100% is offline. Teams of six move along corridor lines, cannot pass through damaged nodes and must build a path of undamaged nodes to reach remote damage. They can run autonomously, which the manual jokes may repair the bowling alley before the warp drive, or be ordered by click. Casualties shrink a team, and replacements come only by docking ([Manual p.36-37](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf?t=1518791376)). The notes could not find team count per ship, travel speed, per-node repair rate, or whether several teams repair faster; the wiki itself says the last is unknown ([Wiki-Eng](http://artemiswiki.pbworks.com/w/page/39352367/Engineering)).

**EmptyEpsilon's version.** Each system has a power slider with the same trade (above 100% more output, more heat, more draw), but coolant is described as an unlimited reserve of which only a limited percentage can be applied at once. System state runs from -100% to 100%. At 0% or below the system stops until repaired, and crews are sent to the room containing the damaged system ([Engineering](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/daid/EmptyEpsilon/wiki/Engineering), [Damage Control](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/daid/EmptyEpsilon/wiki/Damage-Control), [Power Management](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/daid/EmptyEpsilon/wiki/Power-Management)). Coolant cap, heat rates, repair rate and crew sizes are not on the wiki pages the notes read. The "Engineering+" combined station adds shield toggle and calibration to Engineering ([Engineering+](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/daid/EmptyEpsilon/wiki/Engineering-(plus))).

**Experience.** Engineering is "somewhat optional": it runs on autopilot without an engineer, and a good one "can double or even triple the speed and firepower" ([Wiki-Eng](http://artemiswiki.pbworks.com/w/page/39352367/Engineering)). In a playtest report, Engineering was a "secondary need" station whose power stayed at 100% for most of the mission ([Board Game Quest](https://www.boardgamequest.com/artemis-spaceship-bridge-simulator-review/)). The notes' first-person accounts of Engineering workload were not retrievable, so its moment-to-moment loop (watch heat, answer callouts, move pips, prioritise nodes, reset on overheat) is the notes' synthesis rather than a sourced description.

## Transfer to FTLite: copy the structure, replace the interface

**What transfers.** The notes' inferences, which are design hypotheses and not sourced claims, converge on the following. (1) A neutral baseline plus boost or shed per system maps onto FTL-style bars if coarsened to roughly 0 / 1x / 2x / 3x levels, with the shedding order standing in for Artemis's zero-everything fallback. (2) A shared finite cooling resource turns boosting into a real tradeoff. (3) Multi-room systems with partial effectiveness and damaged rooms that block paths already match FTLite's walk-to-hazard repair. (4) Crew injured by overheating in the room they stand in gives the Artemis "injuring crew members in the vicinity" rule a physical analogue. (5) Energy as a ship-wide budget with a refill event (docking) gives a campaign clock. (6) "Ask Engineering for power" callouts, tied to an information gift from another station (scan data, arc, ordnance supply), make players hand things off physically or verbally.

**What changes shape.** FTLite replaces NPC repair teams with players. The Artemis decision "which team to dispatch" becomes "who leaves their console", which is a stronger cost than any Artemis team order. The autonomous-versus-manual toggle is a reusable pattern: a slow, low-priority AI helper as the safe default, with player repair as the high-skill, high-reward mode. EmptyEpsilon's room-as-system model is the closest fit to FTLite's map. Artemis's "only the engineer sees the full damage graph" tension disappears on a shared screen, so any private information has to be created by gating readouts by console or room (Science data visible only when seated at Science).

**What does not transfer.** Separate-screen secrecy cannot exist when couch players see each other and one screen. A Captain with no station would leave a player with no verbs in a 1 to 4 player game, so every player stays operational and leadership emerges. Slider-dense UI and keyboard preset banks are workarounds for per-player consoles and keyboards. Mission scripting and a Game Master are heavy setup, whereas authored encounter sets plus an adaptive director suit casual play. Multiple ships and 6-plus crew assumptions are out of scope. These are the notes' judgments.

## Crew size, solo play and pacing decide whether stations stay busy

**Crew size.** Wikipedia lists Captain, Helm and Weapons as required and Engineering, Science, Comms and Fighter as optional, for up to eight players ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis:_Spaceship_Bridge_Simulator)). The manual's full crew is six players on six or seven computers, with one server supporting up to 36 officers on six ships ([Manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf)). Real solo Artemis is contested: Wikipedia's wording suggests one crew per ship, a Steam page translation says single-player is unsupported, and BoardGameGeek lists a minimum of one, but those last two were seen only via search summaries ([Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/247350/Artemis_Spaceship_Bridge_Simulator?l=dutch)). A review says fewer players can rotate stations and that Comms and Science are easy to pick up ([Board Game Quest](https://www.boardgamequest.com/artemis-spaceship-bridge-simulator-review/)). The manual notes that one computer can host several consoles for a small crew.

EmptyEpsilon's scaling is explicit. The best experience is six players, but 3 to 4 players use Tactical (Helms plus Weapons), Engineering+ and Operations (Science plus Comms), and one person can select multiple roles on one machine and switch with a top-right button ([Site](https://daid.github.io/EmptyEpsilon/), [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1907040)). The multi-role switch claim is a secondary source. Single Pilot merges several stations but loses beam target and frequency setting, shield frequency change, probes and hacking ([Single Pilot](https://github-wiki-see.page/m/daid/EmptyEpsilon/wiki/Single-Pilot)). Star Trek Bridge Crew's AI crew could not follow detailed multi-part orders, and solo micromanagement drained the fun and made the campaign noticeably harder ([Road to VR](https://www.roadtovr.com/star-trek-bridge-crew-review/)). The lesson is that merging or switching works better than competent-AI crew.

**Implication for FTLite.** FTLite has 7 consoles for 1 to 4 players, so it sits closer to EmptyEpsilon's multi-role reality than to Artemis's one-person-per-station. The walk between rooms plays the role of the station-switch button, and walk time becomes the balancing knob. Unmanned consoles could run in a capped auto mode (visible efficiency penalty) and Doors or Life Support could be partly automated at low player counts. These are inferences from the notes.

**Difficulty and pacing.** Artemis difficulty is a configurable scenario option scaled "all the way up to 11", and higher levels make enemies tougher rather than smarter. The manual's setup example for Elite Co-op doubles enemy speed and beam range and damage ([Fistful of Wits](https://fistfulofwits.com/2013/07/10/artemis-bridge-simulator-offers-arch-entertainment/), [Manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf)). Base energy drain also scales with difficulty from 0.6 at level 1 to 5.9 at level 11, which is the direct Engineering link ([Wiki-Energy](http://artemiswiki.pbworks.com/w/page/43660763/Energy)). EmptyEpsilon difficulty seems to live mainly in scenarios and minigame grid sizes (Simple, Normal, Difficult, Fiendish), though the notes did not confirm any global enemy multiplier. The recurring criticism is uneven load. Helm is busiest, Engineering and Science idle, Comms goes mundane, and predictable enemies create a mid-to-late lull ([Board Game Quest](https://www.boardgamequest.com/artemis-spaceship-bridge-simulator-review/)). The Spaceteam model scales differently, by raising the rate of instructions and stacking hazards, with timers as the difficulty tiers (3, 5 or 7 minutes) ([Episodic Content](https://episodiccontentmag.com/2016/01/29/everybodyshake2/), [Spaceteam FAQ](https://www.playspaceteam.com/faq)).

The notes' inferences for FTLite are that raising enemy stats scales damage but not coordination demand, so scale concurrent crises and shorten timers by player count. Route events so each console has a peak moment (fire needs Life Support and Doors, boarding needs Medbay, anomalies need Science). Vary encounters to avoid lulls. Teach by doing with a low-stakes first encounter. Let adjacent players assist a console at partial effect so idle players have a reason to help.

## Teamwork pressure comes from split control, shared stakes and a clock

Spaceteam's creator traced the game to the idea of something shown on one screen that you must react to on another, and notes that if one player falters everyone is slowed, so players help each other ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/everybody-shake-the-making-of-spaceteam-chapter-1-of-3), [Episodic Content](https://episodiccontentmag.com/2016/01/29/everybodyshake2/)). In Artemis the pressure comes from interlocks (Science to Weapons, Helm to Weapons) and from a Captain who must name who is being addressed and give unambiguous orders ([Manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/247350/manuals/Artemis_Manual_1.70-rev5.pdf)). The Science player was kept engaged by constant questions from others ([Board Game Quest](https://www.boardgamequest.com/artemis-spaceship-bridge-simulator-review/)). The manual also warns about friendly fire from unlocked torpedoes, mines and nukes. For FTLite the notes argue the risk is the opposite of the networked games: players sit together and see everything, so they may simply act without asking. Gating information and actions by room or console, plus a shared urgent timer (the Spaceteam card game lesson, a secondhand summary of [Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/turning-spaceteam-card-game-doesnt-suck/)), are the levers. All of this is inference, since no source analyses shared-screen bridge-sim design directly.

## Gaps, version differences and unverified claims

The central caveat is version. The Artemis manual is v1.70 rev5, and 2.x (2013 onward) changed or added features that were not checked; the wiki pages mix versions from roughly 1.6x to 2.x, are undated, and the Engineering preset tool is dated to a 2017 build of 1.5.101 per a search summary ([Rigbook](https://cattail.nu/artemis/engineersRigbook/about.php)). Fandom and pbworks wiki main pages, the Artemis official site and several reviews (Tom's Guide, PC Gamer, Co-Optimus) could not be read. The Thomas Robertson interviews exist ([Space Game Junkie #28](https://spacegamejunkie.com/featured/sgj-podcast-28-treasure-trove-thomas-robertson-i-e-kinda-talk-artemis/amp), [#429](https://www.spacegamejunkie.com/podcasts/sgj-podcast-429-artemis-cosmos/)) but their content was not read, so no design-intent statement should be attributed to him. Missing numbers include beam cooldowns, damage values, scan durations, sensor ranges, exact heat rates, overheat thresholds, team counts, travel and repair speeds, and EmptyEpsilon defaults. The notes also carry several internally conflicting or weak claims: start energy 999 versus 1000, the coolant table columns for 9 and 10 units exceeding the standard 8 (likely a spreadsheet or variant), Artemis solo-play support, the Starship Horizons and Artemis Cosmos player counts (search summaries only), the Spaceteam 2-8 versus 2-4 conflict, and a reviewer's remark that the demo used automatic damage control (a search summary only). Several claims in the notes had no source link and are omitted here: the Captain's Map details beyond the manual, the specific Skaraan cloak and warp-burst timings, and the Bridge Crew voice-command update beyond its headline. EmptyEpsilon figures come from a mirror of the GitHub wiki and were summarised by a small model, and no EmptyEpsilon source code was read, which suits the GPL constraint.

## Conclusion

The design insight to carry into FTLite is that Artemis stations are split by information flow, not by ship system, and that is why shrinking the crew just merges consoles. FTLite's 7 consoles for 1 to 4 players already puts it in that regime. The walking crew member is simultaneously EmptyEpsilon's station-switch button and Artemis's damage-control team, so walk distance is the one knob that sets solo difficulty, repair cost and the price of leaving a console.

Engineering is the highest-value lift, with one caution from the evidence. The neutral-plus-boost dial with shared coolant and node damage fits FTLite's power bars and shedding order, but Artemis's own playtests found Engineering idle at 100%, and its presets show that real-time power control collapses to a few discrete states. FTLite therefore needs encounters that force power decisions and repair walks onto every console in rotation, with concurrent demand rather than enemy stats as the difficulty knob. All of that remains a hypothesis until it is playtested at each player count with per-console idle time tracked.
