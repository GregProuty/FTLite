# CC0 packs cover FTLite's effects, UI and crew; draw the ship

Open-licence pixel art can supply roughly two-thirds of FTLite's visual needs without any attribution burden, but not the part that defines the game's look. Fire, smoke, explosions, muzzle flashes and beam effects are fully covered by **CC0 packs from Reactorcore, Aim Studios, ansimuz, Limofeus and Kenney**; HUD panels, bars and buttons by **Buch's and Kenney's CC0 pixel UI kits**; button prompts by **Kenney's CC0 1-bit input prompts**; and text by **CC0 fonts m5x7 and monogram** or OFL families such as VT323 and Tiny5. Crew is workable: **Puny Characters (CC0, 16 px on a 32 px canvas, 8 directions, Hurt and Death animations)** is the only small open pack that ships a downed pose, and at 2x it sits exactly on FTLite's 32 px tile. The weak spot is the ship interior itself: the only CC0 sci-fi interior tilesets at 16 px (Buch's "Sci-fi Interior tiles", the MurphysDad/HaywardMorihara "Sci-Fi Facility" pack) are clean and cartoonish, the one open set with a real animated sliding door (Spaceyyy's "Cozy Space Interiors") is CC-BY 4.0 and deliberately cozy, and **no open pack at all contains hull-breach or wall-damage decals, a sitting pose, oxygen-venting sprites, electric arcs, or ship-system hazard icons**. Those, plus the scrappy palette that makes a Firefly-tone ship read as lived-in, must be drawn in-house, most cheaply by recolouring and extending a CC0 base. Legally the path is clear: CC0 and CC-BY are safe to bundle in a sold Electron build and to commit to a public repository; CC-BY-SA is safe only when shipped unmodified; GPL-only art, non-commercial, "no redistribution" itch packs, blank-licence itch pages and anything tagged Generative AI should be excluded. Spec §12's "original art and audio only" should become "original by default; third-party only under an allow-list, recorded per file in a manifest that generates the credits screen and a THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES file."

## Open interior tilesets exist but none is scrappy, and none has breach decals

The survey found about six genuinely CC0 or CC-BY top-down sci-fi interior sets, and the CC0 ones are small. Buch's **Sci-fi Interior tiles** (CC0, 2014) is a single 448x192 sheet, which is 28x12 tiles at 16 px, and the author records that the commissioner "found them too cartoonish for their purposes" ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/sci-fi-interior-tiles)); Nikles's **47-autotile edition** repackages the same art for autotiling on a 512x256 sheet, also CC0 ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/sci-fi-interior-tiles-47-autotile-edition)). The **Sci-Fi Facility Asset Pack** (MurphysDad art, CC0 on both OGA and itch) adds facility wall and floor tiles plus animated computers, electric gates, buttons and computer-menu overlays, which is the closest CC0 source for console sprites, though its theme is laboratory rather than freighter ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/sci-fi-facility-asset-pack); [itch.io](https://murphysdad.itch.io/sci-fi-facility)). Hyptosis's **Space Scifi Rpg Tiles 48x48** (CC0) has the most lived-in colony/station/spaceship scope and the warmest hand-drawn look, but 48 px does not scale cleanly into a 32 px grid and the author himself warns it "won't work with some of my other sets" ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/node/80030)); his 24x24 cyberpunk set (CC0) has the same resampling problem ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/24-x-scifi-cyberpunk-tiles)).

The only open set with a proper door is Spaceyyy's **Cozy Space Interiors**: 12 floors, a 9-slice rounded room frame, eight 16x32 front walls (window, screen, vent, pipes, wall light, sign) and a sliding door in closed, open and 4-frame opening states, on a 16 px grid with Tiled files and 1x/2x/4x PNGs ([itch.io](https://aquumgifts.itch.io/cozy-space-interiors)). Its licence depends on where you get it: the **OpenGameArt upload is CC-BY 4.0** with the mandated credit line `"Cozy Space Interiors" by Spaceyyy (https://aquumgifts.itch.io/cozy-space-complete), CC-BY 4.0` ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/cozy-space-interiors-16-px-top-down-sci-fi-tileset)), while the itch edition and the wider 16-pack Cozy Space Complete bundle use a house licence that forbids redistributing the assets themselves ([Cozy Space Complete](https://aquumgifts.itch.io/cozy-space-complete)). Use the OGA copy. Among the remaining CC-BY options, Unnamed's **32x32 Sci-Fi tileset** is dual-licensed CC-BY 4.0 / CC-BY-SA 4.0, so the BY option can be chosen ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/32x32-sci-fi-tileset)), but it is native 32 px and would clash in density with 2x-scaled 16 px crew.

Several attractive itch packs fail the licence test. TheRealFanMan's 32x32 interior is the only set found with a damaged floor tile and a three-state door, but its terms forbid redistributing the original files and it is not a CC licence ([itch.io](https://therealfanman.itch.io/top-down-sci-fi-interior)); Aske4's 32x32 station tiles have no licence on the page at all, only a comment permitting commercial use ([itch.io](https://aske4.itch.io/free-space-station)); SteelSoldier's set requires a link credit and grants no redistribution right ([itch.io](https://steelsoldier.itch.io/top-down-sci-fi-tileset)). Space Station 13 and Space Station 14 hold enormous 32 px station art including breach, fire and damage overlays, but /tg/station's assets are **CC-BY-SA 3.0** ([tgstation README](https://github.com/tgstation/tgstation/blob/master/README.md)) and SS14 mixes CC-BY-SA 3.0 with some **CC-BY-NC-SA** assets that must be removed for commercial use, tracked per asset in `meta.json` files ([SS14 README](https://github.com/space-wizards/space-station-14/blob/master/README.md)). "LPC goes to space" borrows Hyptosis's palette but is CC-BY-SA 4.0 / GPL 3.0, so it mixes visually with the CC0 set and not legally ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/lpc-goes-to-space)). Kenney, usually the first stop for CC0, has no sci-fi interior tileset: its Sci-Fi RTS pack is exterior base-building and its 1-bit and roguelike packs are fantasy/generic ([Kenney Sci-Fi RTS](https://kenney.nl/assets/sci-fi-rts)).

The practical reading is that no open pack delivers the Firefly tone; the only set advertising "junkers" is coldrice's paid, non-CC 16x16 pack ([itch.io](https://coldrice.itch.io/16x16-sci-fi-tiles)). The realistic route is a CC0 base (Buch or Facility at 2x, optionally Cozy Space Interiors' door and wall variants under CC-BY) recoloured toward rust and ochre in Aseprite, with in-house grime, patch and breach decals layered on top. Breach and wall-damage decals must be drawn in any case: none exist under CC0 or CC-BY in the surveyed packs.

## Puny Characters is the only CC0 crew with a downed frame

No open pack is simultaneously top-down, civilian sci-fi, 16 to 32 px, four-direction, and complete with downed and sitting poses, but one comes close. **16x16 Puny Characters** by Shade is CC0 on both itch and OGA, drawn on a 32x32 canvas with a 16x16 footprint, 8-directional, with Idle, Walk, Sword/Bow/Stave Attack, Throw, Hurt and Death animations and no fixed palette, so a hue-shift for per-player tinting is unobstructed ([itch.io](https://merchant-shade.itch.io/16x16-puny-characters); [OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/puny-characters)). The Death animation's last frame doubles as a static downed sprite, Throw reads as a repair or interact gesture, and the 32 px canvas leaves room for carried tools. Two caveats: the paid Editor Edition is not CC0 ([itch.io](https://merchant-shade.itch.io/16x16-puny-character-plus-sprites)), and the characters are fantasy classes that need clothing recolours to read as deckhands. **Ninja Adventure** (Pixel-Boy and AAA, CC0) offers 50-plus 16x16 characters including humans and robots, but the itch page does not itemise animations and the four-direction claim rests on an undisputed user comment ([itch.io](https://pixel-boy.itch.io/ninja-adventure-asset-pack)). **15 Top-Down Character Sprites** (CC0, 16 and 24 px, four-direction walk only) ([itch.io](https://piano-no-renshu.itch.io/top-down-character-sprites)), ArMM1998's **Zelda-like** pack with a character template (CC0) ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/zelda-like-tilesets-and-sprites)) and isaiah658's plain-clothes modern adults and children (CC0) ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/isaiah658s-pixel-pack-1)) are secondary CC0 sources of civilian bodies.

On the CC-BY side, Antifarea's **Twelve 16x18 RPG sprites plus base** (CC-BY 3.0) includes townspeople and a base template, with a mandatory credit to Charles Gabriel and a link back to OpenGameArt.org ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/twelve-16x18-rpg-sprites-plus-base)); Sharm's **Tiny 16: Basic** (CC-BY 4.0, DawnBringer 16 palette) ships a separate `dead.png` with dead and sleeping characters but only 2-frame animations ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/tiny-16-basic)). Spaceyyy's **Cozy Space Station** (CC-BY 4.0) is the only open set that is literally sci-fi crew, eight characters with matching workstations and tools on the shared 16 px palette, but its "39 transparent sprites" wording implies the crew are static, which was not resolved ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/node/194876)). The Universal LPC generator is a mature open tool with sit, run and emote poses in the LPC Revised base, but output is 64x64 (two FTLite tiles tall), downscaling pixel art 50% ruins outlines, and the combined licence is CC-BY-SA 4.0+/GPL 3.0+ with a credits sheet listing every contributing artist ([ULPC GitHub](https://github.com/LiberatedPixelCup/Universal-LPC-Spritesheet-Character-Generator); [LPC Revised](https://opengameart.org/content/lpc-revised-character-basics)). It is the wrong density and the wrong licence for this project.

Two gaps are unavoidable. **No open 16 to 32 px pack has a sitting frame**; the practical fake is cropping the lower body behind the console sprite over an idle frame, or drawing one prone and one seated frame per base body, which at 16 px is a few dozen pixels of work. **Pixel density must match across the scene**: a 16 px crew pack at 2x beside a 16 px tileset at 2x (Puny Characters over Buch or Facility tiles) gives one density, whereas a native 32 px tileset under 2x characters would show the "chunky character on fine floor" mismatch. Outline style (hard black versus selective) could not be read from page text for any pack and needs a side-by-side visual check before committing.

## Effects, UI, prompts and fonts are solved under CC0

The effects layer is the best-served category. Reactorcore's **Fire Smoke Animations** (OGA, July 2025, CC0) supplies pixel fire and smoke "in multiple variants, styles and sizes" as sheets and individual PNGs ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/fire-smoke-animations)), and his companion **Gun Muzzle Flash Effects** (CC0) adds yellow fire and blue ion flashes plus plasma and blade slashes, about 232 sprites ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/gun-muzzle-flash-effects-fire-and-ion-and-melee)). Aim Studios' **Explosions - Pixel Art** (CC0) gives 32x32 blasts and explosions in default and blue variants plus 64x64 "realistic" sets ([itch.io](https://aim-studios.itch.io/explosions-pixel-art)); BitingChaos's five-frame 16x16 Pico-8 explosion is CC0 ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/16x16-explosion)); ansimuz's **Warped Shooting FX** (CC0) covers 12 beam, ray and electric shot effects ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/warped-shooting-fx)); stealthix's Animated Fires (CC0) adds three fire styles in three colours ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/animated-fires)). Limofeus's **Pixel simulations** (CC0) is the nearest thing to a venting sprite, with gas leak and gas explosion sequences at 64x64 and 16x16 ([itch.io](https://limofeus.itch.io/pixel-simulations)), and Kenney's Smoke Particles and Particle Pack (CC0, 70 and 80 files) give soft pre-rendered puffs for a PixiJS emitter when downsampled with nearest filtering ([Kenney Smoke Particles](https://kenney.nl/assets/smoke-particles); [Kenney Particle Pack](https://kenney.nl/assets/particle-pack)). Two effects remain unfilled: no verified CC0 pixel-art lightning or electric-arc sheet was found (the itch candidates show no licence in listings) ([itch.io lightning tag](https://itch.io/game-assets/tag-lightning/tag-pixel-art)), and oxygen venting has no dedicated sprite, so a tinted smoke puff or a procedural Graphics line is the route for both.

For HUD chrome, Kenney's **Pixel UI pack (750 assets)** (CC0, 2015, panels to buttons) ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/pixel-ui-pack-750-assets)) and Buch's **Sci-fi User Interface** and **UI pieces** (CC0, DawnBringer palette, fill bars, windows, checkboxes, health and mana bars, portrait and icon slots) ([Sci-fi UI](https://opengameart.org/content/sci-fi-user-interface); [UI pieces](https://opengameart.org/content/ui-pieces)) give recolourable 9-slice material with zero legal overhead, and Zed Hanok's CC0 LCARS-style kit of 24x24 tiles suits the in-world console screens precisely because it is tiled rather than windowed ([itch.io](https://zed-hanok.itch.io/lcars-ui-pixel-art)). The one kit already shaped like a spaceship HUD, heyheythere's **Pixel UI Sci-Fi Free**, with hull, shield, energy and stamina bar fills, 18 tintable 16x16 icons, gauges, cooldown sweeps and a bundled BMFont, is CC-BY 4.0 with the requested line "Pixel UI Sci-Fi Free by heyheythere - https://heyheythere.itch.io/pixel-ui-scifi-free - CC BY 4.0" ([itch.io](https://heyheythere.itch.io/pixel-ui-scifi-free)). Kenney's vector-style UI Pack - Sci-Fi is CC0 but not pixel art ([kenney.nl](https://kenney.nl/assets/ui-pack-sci-fi)). Avoid Veyroa's "Free CC0" fantasy UI pack: the title claims CC0, the page has no licence section and credits other creators whose packs carry their own terms ([itch.io](https://veyroa.itch.io/fantasy-minimal-pixel-art-gui)).

Icons split cleanly between prompts, which are solved, and hazards, which are not. Kenney's **1-Bit Pixel Input Prompts 16x** (CC0, over 800 sprites covering keyboard, Xbox, Nintendo, PlayStation, Steam and generic pads) is exactly what a four-pad couch game needs ([itch.io](https://kenney-assets.itch.io/1-bit-pixel-input-prompts-16)). Nikoichu's **1-bit Pixel Icons** (CC0, 1,476 16x16 icons) covers medicine, tools and warfare ([itch.io](https://nikoichu.itch.io/pixel-icons)), and game-icons.net offers roughly 4,180 vector icons including flame, blaster and burn glyphs, but under **CC BY 3.0 with per-author credit** across about 50 contributors and no bulk PNG export yet ([game-icons.net about](https://game-icons.net/about.html); [fire tag](https://game-icons.net/tags/fire.html)). No open set contains oxygen, hull-breach, door or sensor glyphs, and FTLite's hazard set is about ten 16x16 icons, so drawing them is cheaper than licensing and avoids the CC BY 3.0 per-icon bookkeeping.

Fonts carry the least risk. **m5x7** by Daniel Linssen (CC0, nominal 16 px) ([itch.io](https://managore.itch.io/m5x7)) and **monogram** by datagoblin (CC0, 5 px wide, with a ready bitmap PNG+JSON) ([itch.io](https://datagoblin.itch.io/monogram)) avoid every licensing question. The Google Fonts pixel families Press Start 2P, VT323, Silkscreen, Pixelify Sans and Tiny5 are all SIL OFL 1.1, which permits bundling in commercial games while requiring the copyright statement, licence notice and licence text to ship ([OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)); only Press Start 2P declares a Reserved Font Name ([OFL.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/fonts/main/ofl/pressstart2p/OFL.txt)), which matters because the OFL FAQ "normally" treats a format change as a modification that may not carry the RFN. Baking a BMFont atlas for PixiJS 8's BitmapText, which loads AngelCode .fnt files and can install a font from a TTF with `scaleMode: 'nearest'` ([PixiJS 8 BitmapText guide](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/components/scene-objects/text/bitmap); [BitmapFont API](https://pixijs.download/release/docs/text.BitmapFont.html)), is such a format change, so either rename the exported atlas or prefer the RFN-free and CC0 families. Not every itch font is permissive: KitBit Regular is CC BY-NC ([itch.io](https://magikya.itch.io/kitbit-regular-font)).

## Eleven things the team must draw itself

Consolidating the gaps across all four surveys gives a bounded in-house art list, and most items are small because they extend CC0 bases rather than start from nothing. The interior needs **a scrappy recolour of the chosen 16 px base** toward rust, ochre and patched metal, since every open set is clean; **hull-breach and wall-damage decals** and **damaged floor variants**, which no CC0 or CC-BY pack contains; **a ship-door set** if the CC-BY Cozy Space door is rejected on tone or credit grounds, since Facility's electric gates are unverified as doors; and **freighter-specific consoles and props** (helm, weapons, engine, medbay, cargo) beyond Facility's lab computers. The crew needs **one seated frame and one prone frame per base body** and **uniform recolours** to turn Puny Characters' fantasy classes into deckhands. Effects need **a 3 to 5 frame 16x16 electric arc** and **an oxygen-venting puff**, both absent from open packs. UI needs **about ten hazard and system glyphs** (fire, O2, breach, power, repair, medical, door, weapons, sensors, shields). Everything else, including fire, smoke, explosions, muzzle flashes, beams, panels, bars, prompts and fonts, can be pulled from CC0 sources and merely palette-matched.

The order of work follows from density and style dependencies: pick the base tileset and crew pack first and check outlines side by side at 2x, then recolour both to one master palette, and only then draw the in-house pieces against that palette. Every open pack's outline convention is unknown from page text alone, and Puny Characters' Death frame count and Ninja Adventure's animation list need confirmation from the downloaded zips before either is adopted.

## Licence mechanics decide what may ship and what may be committed

The licence categories behave very differently inside a sold, closed-source Electron build that might later be open-sourced. **CC0** works "may be copied, modified, distributed ... without asking, crediting or notifying the creating artist" and commercial use is fine ([OpenGameArt FAQ](https://opengameart.org/content/faq)); Kenney adds only that its logo is reserved ([kenney.nl/support](https://kenney.nl/support)). **CC-BY 4.0** requires retaining the creator's identification, copyright notice, licence notice and a link where practicable, and indicating any modification, but allows this "in any reasonable manner based on the medium," including a link to a credits page ([CC BY-SA 4.0 legal code, same attribution text](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)); 4.0 also reinstates rights automatically if a violation is cured within 30 days, which 3.0 licences such as Antifarea's and game-icons.net's lack, and 3.0 additionally requires the work's title ([CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)). OpenGameArt reads CC's anti-technical-measures clause as barring CC-BY and BY-SA art from DRM-wrapped stores and notes Steam may or may not count depending on packaging ([OpenGameArt FAQ](https://opengameart.org/content/faq)); this is OGA's interpretation, not CC's text, but it is a reason to prefer CC0 and OGA-BY (CC-BY minus the DRM clause) where a storefront is in view.

**CC-BY-SA** is narrower than its reputation. The CC FAQ states that "ShareAlike applies only to adaptations" and that "a collection or compilation does not have to be released under SA," so an unmodified BY-SA sheet bundled in FTLite does not pull the code or the original art under BY-SA, whereas any sheet the team recolours, re-cuts or composites becomes Adapted Material that must ship under BY-SA ([CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/); [CC BY-SA 4.0 §3(b)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)). Since FTLite's whole plan is to recolour everything to one palette, BY-SA art is effectively unusable here, which rules out SS13, SS14 and the LPC family. **GPL-only art** is worse: the FSF says the GPL can apply to any work and that a GPL'd game's art may be licensed separately ([GNU GPL FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#WhatCaseIsOutputGPL)), but gives no guidance on a GPL sprite sheet inside a proprietary program, and OGA warns non-GPL projects to "fully understand" the risk ([OpenGameArt FAQ](https://opengameart.org/content/faq)). Because Electron packs assets into app.asar with the code, the FSF's "same executable file" test for a combined work ([GNU GPL FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#MereAggregation)) points the wrong way; exclude GPL-only art unless the code itself goes GPL, and where an asset is dual-licensed, pick the CC option, which OGA confirms is permitted ("You must follow only one of the licenses") ([OpenGameArt FAQ](https://opengameart.org/content/faq)). CC licences are irrevocable, so a copy downloaded under CC0 stays CC0 even if the artist later changes the listing ([CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)).

The traps are mostly about whether the uploader had the right to grant the tag. OGA moderates uploads and removes art at the author's request "regardless of license," and tells uploaders to be "absolutely certain" of the licence or face removal ([OpenGameArt FAQ](https://opengameart.org/content/faq)), so a stolen sheet re-tagged CC0 was never licensed at all. itch.io does not force or verify licence selection ([itch.io post 687409](https://itch.io/post/687409/view-in-topic)), which is why Aske4-style blank fields and seller-written "no redistribution" clauses are common. CC licences expressly exclude trademark rights ([CC BY 4.0 §2(b)(2)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)), so any Serenity silhouette or FTL-UI pastiche is a trade-dress question no CC tag can cure, which aligns with spec §12's existing Firefly rule. OGA has frozen AI-generated submissions pending litigation ([OGA node 175700](https://opengameart.org/node/175700)) and itch requires a mandatory Generative AI disclosure tag ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/software/platforms/indie-distribution-platform-itch-io-now-requires-asset-creators-to-disclose-the-use-of-generative-ai-in-their-work)); for a possibly commercial game, exclude tagged packs and anything with no named human author.

Provenance should be recorded once, per file, in a machine-readable manifest that generates everything else. The content standard is CC's TASL (Title, Author, Source, License) plus a modifications note, with the warning that crediting the hosting site instead of the author, or burying credit in metadata, is a known failure ([CC wiki: Best practices for attribution](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Best_practices_for_attribution)); OGA's suggested line is `"[asset name]" by [author name] licensed [license(s)]: [asset url]` ([OpenGameArt FAQ](https://opengameart.org/content/faq)). The best shipped precedent is Endless Sky's Debian-format `copyright` file, where every image glob carries Copyright, License (GPL-3+, CC-BY-SA-4.0, CC-BY-4.0, CC0 and more side by side) and a Comment with the source URL and edits such as "Cropped and edited" ([endless-sky/copyright](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/endless-sky/endless-sky/master/copyright)); 0 A.D. does the same with a top-level LICENSE.txt and per-directory licence files splitting GPL code from CC-BY-SA art ([0ad/LICENSE.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0ad/0ad/master/LICENSE.txt)). For FTLite, an `assets/CREDITS.toml` with fields for paths, title, author as requested, source URL, SPDX licence id, licence URL, retrieval date, archived page copy, modifications and verbatim attribution notice should be the single source of truth, with a CI check that fails on any file under `assets/` lacking an entry or carrying a disallowed licence, and a build step that emits both the in-game credits screen and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.txt` so they cannot drift. OFL fonts need their copyright line and OFL.txt in that notices file ([OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)); game-icons.net, if used, needs a generated per-icon author list because CC BY 3.0 demands title and author per work and has no cure period ([game-icons.net about](https://game-icons.net/about.html); [CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)).

The spec §12 line "Use original art and audio only" should be replaced with four rules. First, original art and audio remain the default and every third-party file is an exception justified in the manifest. Second, bundled third-party art and fonts are allowed only under CC0, CC-BY 3.0/4.0, OGA-BY, or OFL 1.1; CC-BY-SA only for files shipped byte-for-byte unmodified; and never GPL-only, NC, ND, "free for personal use", untagged or blank-licence itch downloads, or anything tagged Generative AI, with GPL-only revisited if the code is ever GPL'd. Third, each adoption requires the OGA or itch licence field read on the primary page, the author-equals-uploader check, the attribution notice and comments read, the downloaded file compared with the preview (OGA says previews may be all-rights-reserved), and the page archived with URL, date and screenshot. Fourth, the credits screen, the notices file and the store page listing are generated from the manifest, and the existing §12 trademark rule is extended to cover third-party art that depicts protected designs.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the usual assumption about open game art: the generic layers (effects, UI chrome, prompts, fonts, even crew bodies) are abundant under CC0, while the one thing a spaceship game most needs, a coherent lived-in interior, is exactly where the open ecosystem is thinnest and where the only complete sets are copyleft station simulators. That makes "original art only" the wrong rule and "original ship, borrowed everything else" the right one. Spending the team's pixel budget on the hull, its scars, its doors and its consoles, over a CC0 base recoloured to a single master palette, buys the Firefly tone while the fire, smoke and HUD arrive for free.

The second implication is that licence hygiene is cheaper to build than to retrofit. A dozen-field manifest and a CI check cost an afternoon now; a BY-SA sheet that got recoloured, a GPL sprite found in app.asar, or a CC BY 3.0 icon with no title credit costs far more once the game is sold. Adopt the allow-list and the manifest before the first third-party PNG lands in the repository, and the "possibly open-sourced later" option stays open with no asset ever needing to be pulled.
